Bench notes / audio

A 12 V stereo class-AB amp with its own discrete supply, on one fab panel

What the one-transistor amp became: per channel a BC547 differential pair, a BC557 voltage amplifier with a bootstrapped load, a trimmable Vbe multiplier and BD139/BD140 followers on a 12 V rail. The midpoint sits at 5.93 V for any transistor gain, Zin is 43k so a 10k log pot works, quiescent current is 21 mA, and it delivers 0.95 W into 8 ohm at 0.5% THD, 1.3 W into 4 ohm at the onset of clipping, 0.8 W into 10 ohm at 0.3% (amp_final.cir, ampB4.cir). The supply is a fuse, 1N5401 bridge, 2 x 2200 uF, a 13 V zener and a BC547 + TIP41C pass pair with a 2 A clamp: 11.6 V at light load, holds regulation to 0.38 A from a 12 VAC transformer and to 1.1 A from 15 VAC, plus a 12 V DC input that bypasses it (psu.cir). Three generated KiCad boards, DRC clean as a 2-layer panel and as single-sided etch versions.

The problem

What the one-transistor stage could not do

The original stage biased by hFE, ran its bias current through the voice coil, clipped above 50 mVpk and had an input impedance of 11 ohm. The brief for the rebuild: stereo, a volume knob, 4 to 10 ohm speakers, BD139 as the baseline output device but any pinout fittable, a separate discrete supply fed from a low-voltage transformer or 12 V DC, test points for beginners, hybrid through-hole / SMD footprints, and one panel for JLCPCB or PCBWay plus a single-sided variant.

The mechanism

A single common-emitter front end with shunt feedback failed two of the three tests that decide whether a small discrete amp is usable: its midpoint moved from 7.8 V to 6.7 V across BC547 hFE 110 to 450, and its gain changed by 22% across the travel of a 10k pot (ampA.cir). A differential pair with divider bias and series feedback passed both (midpoint 5.90 to 5.97 V, gain 20.1 to 18.0 from a 0 to 5k source), so that is the channel.

Figure 1

One amplifier channel

The volume pot appears as the 2.5k source resistance (its mid-travel value). The 1k trimmer sits in series with the 470 ohm of the multiplier; the deck simulates the pair as 820 ohm. Two solder-jumper 3x3 blocks on the board (not drawn) let Q4 and Q5 take any TO-220 / TO-126 / TO-92 pinout.

Figure 2

The supply

Everything discrete. The zener reference is RC-filtered, the pass device is a BC547 driving a TIP41C, and the clamp transistor pulls the driver's base down when 0.65 V appears across the 0.33 ohm sense resistor. D6 lets 12 V DC on the output (from the DC-in terminal through an SB540) back-charge the raw rail instead of breaking the pass transistor's base-emitter junction.

Where the simulation corrected me

Findings

CORRECTED. Two 1N4148s for output bias looked textbook and gave 50 mA quiescent (two diodes at the 10 mA VAS current drop 1.44 V against the 1.22 V two BD139 junctions need); one diode plus a resistor gave 2 uA to 2 mA and 1 to 5% crossover THD. Only a Vbe multiplier lands in between, hence the trimmer (bias table above).

CORRECTED. The first diff-pair version starved at 4 ohm: 0.3 W at 4.5% THD, because the bootstrapped VAS load (2 x 680 ohm, 4 mA) could not sink the PNP's base current. 2 x 270 ohm (about 10 mA) gives 1.3 W (ampB.cir vs ampB2.cir).

LIMIT. Output power follows the output devices' hFE: at hFE 40 the 4 ohm channel clips at 0.2 W, at hFE 250 it makes 1.8 W at 1%. Buy BD139-16 / BD140-16 (hFE 100 to 250), not the ungraded part.

CONFIRMED. Zin 43k and midpoint 5.93 V against hand values of 26k-plus and 5.95 V; -3 dB at 34 Hz into 4 ohm against 1/(2 pi x 1000u x 4.7) = 34 Hz.

DEFECT (fixed). The PSU's first clamp sat on the pass transistor's base and was overpowered by the driver: a short drew 3 A. Moving the clamp to the driver's base (the zener node) limits at 2.1 A; the 0.47 ohm sense also cost 0.5 V at 1 A, so it became 0.33 ohm.

LIMIT. A 12 VAC secondary regulates only to 0.38 A; above that the output follows the raw trough (10.2 V at 1 A). For full stereo power use 15 VAC at 1.5 A or more (30 VA), and a heatsink on the TIP41C (4.9 W at 1.1 A).

Model caveats. BC547/BC557/BD139/BD140 are generic models with datasheet-typical hFE, swept down to datasheet minimums; the TIP41C is a generic BF 60 model. No thermal coupling is modelled: the multiplier transistor must sit next to the output devices (it does on the board) and the 70 C rows above assume everything at one temperature.
Boards

Three generated KiCad boards, two fab packages

  • Amp, 100 x 83 mm, 2 layers: both channels, four output transistors on the top edge with their 3x3 pinout jumper blocks below (row = device pin, column = E / C / B, bridged on the diagonal for BD139/BD140), trimmers, test points TP1/TP2 (+12V, GND) and per channel TPx1 base, TPx2 output, TPx3 bias. 829 track segments, 18 vias, DRC clean.
  • PSU, 90 x 50 mm: AC terminal and fuse on the left, bridge and reservoirs in the middle, TIP41C on the top edge for a heatsink, DC in and two DC out terminals on the right, LED header J5 for a front-panel LED. DRC clean.
  • Volume, 42 x 26 mm: dual 10k log pot (Alps RK097 footprint) with the shaft over the bottom edge, L-G-R headers in and out, and a power LED fed by two wires from the PSU. DRC clean.
  • Panel: 134.2 x 137.2 mm, mouse-bite tabs, DRC clean, gerbers in the release zip. Both fabs treat it as a multi-design panel (small surcharge).
  • Single-sided versions for home etching: amp 120 x 100, PSU 117 x 65, volume 42 x 26 mm; where one copper layer was not enough the build added wire-link footprints JLn (PSU 1, volume 1, amp 14). The volume board is DRC clean; the single-sided PSU has one connection left open (bleeder R1 pin 1 to VRAW: add a wire to TP1); the single-sided amp is routed but keeps 44 DRC errors (open connections and wire links crowding neighbouring copper around the jumper blocks) and needs hand finishing in KiCad before etching.
  • Hybrid footprints: every resistor, ceramic capacitor, small electrolytic and diode has a 1206 / SOD-123 / SMA twin on the same pads; BC547/BC557 positions take SOT-23 on the 2-layer board.
Build notes

Bringing it up

  • Verified (simulated): set each channel's trimmer for 7 to 10 mV between TP3 (E4) and TP2 (OUT): 15 to 20 mA quiescent, 0.2 to 0.5% THD at 50 mW. Start fully anticlockwise.
  • Verified: the DC-in path is safe with no transformer, with the transformer, and with both.
  • Proposed, unsimulated: clip-on heatsinks on the four output devices if you drive 4 ohm speakers hard; a 1 A slow fuse on the AC input; the pot's clockwise end on pin 3 is the datasheet convention, swap the two outer wires if the knob works backwards.
  • Tried and failed: diode bias (see findings), a 0.47 ohm clamp on the pass transistor's base, and a 9 V rail: at 9 V the channel makes 0.33 W into 4 ohm at 1.7% and clips at 0.8 W.
